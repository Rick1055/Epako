# Epako Voice: an honest senior-engineer review

Date: 28 October 2026. Reviewer stance: a senior engineer at a healthcare app company, asked whether this is ready to put in front of patients and clinicians, and what to change.

Everything below was checked against the code, the live site on this laptop and the test results on the day. Where something was not checked, it says so.

---

## The verdict in one paragraph

Epako Voice is an unusually well-built prototype with a real point of view. The backend security work (row-level security attacked on a real Postgres, live attack scripts, rate limits, upload caps, CAPTCHA, account deletion) is better than most early-stage health apps I have reviewed, and the accessibility discipline (contrast measured against the real pixels, axe on every page, big targets, plain language) is genuinely good. But it is **not ready for real patients yet**, for three reasons that matter more than everything else: the website sends **no security headers** while keeping the login token where any injected script can read it; the **"correction" step does not measurably correct** dysarthric speech beyond what the speech recogniser already does; and the **entire website is not in version control**. Fix those three and this becomes a strong pilot candidate.

**Overall: 6.5 / 10 as a product for patients; 8.5 / 10 as a student or hackathon project.**

## What is genuinely strong

Credit where it is due; keep all of this.

1. **Security on the server and database.** Every table is protected by row-level security, and `tests/test_rls.py` attacks every migration on a real embedded Postgres. `scripts/attack_live.py` attacks the live Supabase project as an outsider, another patient and an unverified doctor. The API caps request bodies as they arrive, limits audio to 60 s and 10 MB, rate-limits every route, refuses non-audio uploads at the storage bucket, and re-checks the password (with CAPTCHA) before deleting an account. Clinician verification changes are written to an audit table. Un-verifying a clinician cuts their read access at once.
2. **Honesty in the product.** "Not a medical device" on the home page; "Automated measurements, not clinically validated, not a diagnosis" on the clinician screen and in the PDF; "not measured" is stored as empty, never as 0. No invented reviews, ratings or claims. That is exactly the right posture for a health product.
3. **Privacy by design.** No name is collected. The patient's voice is synthesised on your own server (Piper), not sent to a cloud TTS. Data stays in Supabase Mumbai. "Delete everything" exists and works. Breached-password checks use the k-anonymity range API.
4. **Accessibility discipline.** Contrast is measured against the actual pixels of the 3D garden behind the words (a test most teams never think of), axe runs on every public page in four themes and sizes, there is a skip link, focus trapping in the phone menu, reduced-motion and Save-Data both turn the 3D scene off.
5. **A learning loop.** The patient's yes/no on each phrase teaches a per-patient word table (`feedback_engine`), and a vocabulary of the patient's own words can snap uncertain words to the closest one they use. This is the right idea for dysarthria, where every voice is different.
6. **Documentation.** `DESIGN.md` and `docs/contract.md` are thorough and mostly match the code. The latency and hosting analysis in DESIGN.md is measured, not guessed.

## Critical issues (fix before any real patient uses it)

### C1. The website sends no security headers, and the login token is readable by scripts

**What I found.** `curl -D - http://127.0.0.1:3000/` returns only `content-type`, `Vary`, `Date` and `Connection`. There is no Content-Security-Policy, no `X-Content-Type-Options`, no `Referrer-Policy`, no `Permissions-Policy`, no `X-Frame-Options` / `frame-ancestors`, and (once hosted) no HSTS. DESIGN.md rule 23 and rule 35 promise all of these; `server.js` never sets them. At the same time `app/lib/supabase.ts` uses `createClient` with its defaults, which keeps the session (access and refresh token) in `localStorage`.

**Why it matters.** Together these mean a single cross-site-scripting bug, or one compromised npm package, can read the refresh token and take over a patient's or clinician's account for as long as the token lives. A clinician account reads many patients' health data. Without `frame-ancestors`, the site can also be framed for clickjacking (for example, a hidden "delete my data" or "link this clinician" click).

**Change.**
- In `server.js`, add the headers to every response: a strict CSP (scripts only from self plus Turnstile's origin; `connect-src` self plus your Supabase URL; `frame-src` Turnstile only; `frame-ancestors 'none'`; `object-src 'none'`; `base-uri 'self'`), `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, `Permissions-Policy: microphone=(self), camera=(), geolocation=()`, and HSTS when served over HTTPS. Then add a Playwright test that checks them, as rule 23 asks.
- Keep the 3D scene working under the CSP (it uses blob workers and WebGL; test it).
- Consider moving the session to an httpOnly cookie through the page server (Supabase SSR helpers do this). If that is too big for the deadline, the CSP is the minimum, plus a shorter JWT expiry in Supabase.

Effort: half a day for the headers and test; one to two days for cookie sessions.

### C2. The "correction" does not measurably correct dysarthric speech

**What I found.** `outputs/eval_torgo.json` (the full TORGO evaluation, 7,884 clips) shows word error rate before and after the repair step for each speaker:

| Speaker | Word error, recogniser alone | After repair | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 (dysarthric) | 57 % | 57 % | 0 |
| F03 (dysarthric) | 31 % | 31 % | 0 |
| F04 (dysarthric) | 17 % | 17 % | −0.3 points |
| M01 (dysarthric) | 60 % | 60 % | −0.2 points |
| M02 (dysarthric) | 50 % | 50 % | −0.1 points |
| M03 (dysarthric) | 9 % | 9 % | −0.5 points |
| M04 (dysarthric, severe) | 90 % | 80 % | −10 points |
| M05 (dysarthric) | 43 % | 42 % | −0.5 points |
| Control speakers | 11 to 12 % | same | 0 |

Before any learning, the regex repair and confidence handling add essentially nothing except for one severe speaker. Everything the patient experiences as "correction" is the Parakeet recogniser's own accuracy, and for half the dysarthric speakers it gets roughly every second word wrong.

**Why it matters.** The product's promise, "we'll help it be heard", rests on this number. A clinician or an investor will ask for it first. Also, the main dataset (TORGO) is single words and short sentences read aloud, not everyday conversation.

**Change.**
- Say it honestly in the product and the pitch: the recogniser is fine-tuned for dysarthric speech; the personal learning loop is what is meant to close the gap, and show its measured effect. `scripts/simulate_learning.py` exists; put its before/after numbers (held-out speakers, with confidence intervals) next to the table above.
- Make the learning loop the hero: every confirmation and correction should visibly improve the next result ("I've learned 12 of your words").
- Evaluate on the patient's own phrases (the practice prompts), which is the real use, and report per-severity numbers.
- Never show a "spot on" rate as a clinical claim.

**Update (same day): measured.** `docs/EVIDENCE.md` has the learning replay. On one-off words learning adds nothing; on phrases a speaker said 3+ times, the first answer was right +6 to +36 points more often after learning on held-out speakers (simulated perfect feedback, small samples). Progress now shows each patient what has been learned.

### C3. The whole website is not in version control

**What I found.** `git status` shows `web/` as untracked. 76 commits exist, all for the Python side. The entire frontend (routes, 3D scene, asset pipeline, tests), DESIGN.md and today's work exist only on this laptop's disk, which is nearly full.

**Why it matters.** One disk failure, a bad `rm`, or a cleanup script loses weeks of work three days before the deadline. It also means no code review history and no way to roll back.

**Change.** Commit `web/` today, with a `.gitignore` for `web/node_modules`, `web/build`, `web/.cache` and `web/test-results`, and push to a private remote. Keep `epako_sylva_experience.html` and the ponytail submodule out, as decided earlier.

## High priority

### H1. Sign-in is hard for the people this app is for

Dysarthria usually comes with a condition that also affects the hands (Parkinson's, ALS/MND, stroke, cerebral palsy, MS). Today a patient must type an email and a 12-character password and pass a CAPTCHA to get in, and every sign-in on a new device repeats it.

**Change.** Add passkeys (Face ID / fingerprint / phone PIN) or an email magic link as the main way in, keep the password as a fallback, and keep sessions long on the patient's own device. Offer a carer-assisted setup path.

### H2. Clinicians have no two-step sign-in and nobody is signed out when idle

A clinician account reads several patients' health records, often on shared hospital computers. DESIGN.md lists clinician two-step sign-in as "not built yet", and there is no idle timeout anywhere in `web/app`.

**Change.** Require TOTP (Supabase MFA) for clinician accounts before any patient data loads, and sign clinicians out after 15 minutes idle (patients can stay signed in on their own phone).

### H3. The confirm-every-phrase loop is tiring

Every phrase on Speak asks "is this right?" and fixing a wrong one means typing, which is hard for many users (see H1). That cost is paid hundreds of times a day if the app is used for real communication.

**Change.** Speak first, confirm optionally: play the corrected voice straight away when confidence is high, and only ask when the recogniser was unsure (you already have per-word confidence). Let corrections be picked from the top 3 alternatives or from the patient's own word list instead of typed.

### H4. Conversation mode is missing

The backend has a live streaming endpoint (`/ws/rectify`, with per-user socket limits) but no screen uses it. For everyday communication, which is the core need, phrase-by-phrase recording is slow.

**Change.** Build a "talk" mode on the existing WebSocket: hands-free, speaks back each phrase as soon as it ends, big stop button. This is the feature that would most change how useful the product feels.

### H5. Compliance for India

The data is in Mumbai, which is good. But the Privacy page doesn't mention the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, there is no named grievance officer or contact for data requests. Consent to keep recordings is stored (`consent_audio_storage` with `consent_updated_at`) and the storage bucket enforces it, which is good; but only the latest yes/no is kept, with no policy version and no history, so you can't prove which wording someone agreed to or when they withdrew.

**Change.** Keep a consent history (which policy version, when, what was agreed, withdrawal), name a grievance contact on the Privacy page, state retention periods for every kind of data (recordings, transcripts, biomarkers), and write down how a clinician's access is granted and removed. Get a lawyer to read it before a hospital pilot.

### H6. It runs on one laptop

The speech API needs a GPU for good speed (0.35 s median here) and about 4 GB of memory; on the CPU sizes cheap hosting offers it takes 3.5 to 4.4 s per phrase, and a cold start reads 2.4 GB. Today everything (API, page server, dev server) runs on this laptop, which also ran out of memory during testing today and had a background server killed.

**Change.** For the demo, run it from this laptop on mains power with nothing else open, behind a tunnel, as DESIGN.md plans. For a pilot, a small GPU instance (or a CPU instance with a quantised model) with health checks and automatic restarts. Add simple monitoring (uptime, error rate, response time).

## Medium priority

1. **The 3D park can distract and costs battery and data.** It is the most memorable thing about the site, and it is well engineered (instanced, LOD, fallbacks, reduced motion respected). But for someone tired, anxious or with cognitive difficulty after a stroke, a moving, detailed scene behind every screen is a lot. Add a clear "calm mode" switch on first run (not only under Me), default to it on the Speak screen while recording, and measure page weight on a mid-range Android phone on mobile data (the model set is about 16 MB across all tiers; phones load the lighter half).
2. **The far scenery looks game-like on close inspection.** The city blocks, distant people (their lightest model) and identical T-shirt shapes are fine at a glance and in the misted backdrop, but a reviewer who stops and looks will see repetition. Keep the far park blurred by the mist and avoid camera stops that look straight at it.
3. **robots.txt and sitemap.xml are missing** (both 404), though DESIGN.md specifies them. Add them, and the JSON-LD from rule 28.
4. **The Help contact form is not built** (`POST /api/contact` doesn't exist). Patients with speech difficulty can't phone you, so a written channel matters.
5. **Promised garden rewards aren't built.** A leaf on the tree per confirmed phrase, a hellebore opening and a flower per stepping stone are designed but not built; the pages say it in words instead. Either build the one that shows up most (the leaf) or remove the promise.
6. **Accent choice is narrow.** Five American voices and one British. Most likely users in India will want an Indian English voice; add one Piper voice (or a fine-tuned one) before claiming the voice is "yours".
7. **Voice banking doesn't clone the voice.** It picks and tunes the closest stock voice. That is honest in the code; make sure the screen copy says so too, so nobody expects to hear their own old voice.
8. **Biomarkers shown to clinicians are unvalidated.** The disclaimers are good. Before a clinical pilot, compare jitter, shimmer and formant centralisation against a reference tool (Praat) on the same clips and publish the agreement.
9. **Tests depend on the machine.** The browser suite ran 70 false failures today because an orphaned server kept serving an old build, and the parallel run strains memory. Make the Playwright config start its own server (`webServer`) and run with 2 workers locally.
10. **`src/api.py` is 1,051 lines and `World.tsx` 795.** Not urgent, but splitting the API by area (auth, sessions, voice, reports) will make review easier.

## Idea and novelty, honestly

The need is real and under-served: people with dysarthria are routinely misunderstood by people and by voice assistants. But this exact space has strong incumbents:

- **Voiceitt** learns an individual's dysarthric speech and renders it as clear speech, audio or text, and Voiceitt 2 does live conversation and dictation. It is a paid product (reported at about $500 a year) aimed at mild to moderate severity.
- **Google Project Relate** (from Project Euphonia, trained on over a million recordings from more than 1,000 people) offers personalised recognition, "Repeat" in a clear voice, and assistant control, on Android.

What is genuinely different about Epako Voice:

1. **A clinician loop** (link codes, session data, measured voice biomarkers and a PDF report for the speech therapist). Neither incumbent is built around the therapist.
2. **Privacy posture**: no name collected, voice synthesised on your own server, data in India.
3. **A personal, chosen voice** shaped with sliders and voice notes, rather than one stock voice.
4. **Practice as well as communication**: prompts, daily progress, the garden as encouragement.
5. **Free and open models**, so it could reach people who can't pay $500 a year.

Pitch it as "the speech-therapy companion that also helps you be understood", not "we correct dysarthric speech", because the second claim is what Voiceitt and Google already own and the numbers in C2 don't yet support it.

# Epako Voice: UI/UX review

Date: 28 October 2026. Reviewer stance: a UI/UX engineer in a product company, judging smoothness, usability, ease of navigation and mobile fit. The users are people with dysarthria, who are often older, often have a motor impairment (Parkinson's, stroke, ALS/MND, MS, cerebral palsy) and are often tired; the second group is speech therapists.

How it was checked: the production build on this laptop, signed out and signed in (the tour test account), every page at phone size (390 × 844, touch) and desktop size (1440 × 900), with screenshots and measurements taken in the browser: layout shift, main-thread freezes, time until the garden settles, tap targets, text size, sideways scroll, console errors, and frame times while moving between sections. Screenshots are in `web/.cache/review/ux/`.

---

## Verdict

The interface is calm, kind and well-made: plain words everywhere, big primary buttons, nothing jumps around, and the help and privacy pages are some of the best I've seen in a health app. The problems are mostly about **space and attention on phones** and **a few unfinished details**. On a phone, the decorative garden takes the top half of every screen, so the actual task always starts halfway down, the header is crowded to the screen's edge, the bottom tab bar's icons have no labels, and My voice is tucked into an overflow menu with the legal pages. Fix those, and this is a very good patient app.

**Overall: 7 / 10.**

| Area | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Smoothness | 7 / 10 | No layout jumps at all; but every full page load freezes the page for 1 to 3.5 s while the 3D garden is built |
| Usability | 7 / 10 | Clear words, big buttons, forgiving; the voice setup is long, and confirming every phrase is tiring |
| Ease of navigation | 6.5 / 10 | Clear sections on desktop; on phones, unlabelled tab icons and My voice hidden in "More" |
| Mobile fit | 6 / 10 | Nothing overflows and touch targets are mostly right; but half of every screen is decoration, and the header is crowded to the edge |
| Visual design | 8 / 10 | Distinctive, warm, consistent; the misted garden behind text is busy in places |
| Accessibility | 8 / 10 | Contrast tested on real pixels, axe-clean, keyboard paths; motion and motor-impairment gaps remain |
| Writing | 9 / 10 | Plain, warm, specific, never clinical or patronising |

## What works well (keep it)

1. **The writing.** "Say it your way. We'll help it be heard." "Take your time. Say a few phrases whenever you like." "Off means each recording is thrown away once I've understood it." Help questions are written the way a patient would ask them ("It doesn't understand me", "I can't hear my voice"). This is the strongest part of the product.
2. **Nothing moves under your finger.** Layout shift is zero on every page, so a slow, unsteady tap always lands where it was aimed.
3. **One clear primary action per screen.** The coral "Speak" button and the big round microphone are unmistakable, and coral is used for nothing else.
4. **Big controls.** Main buttons are full-width and tall on phones; inputs are large; the password field explains the rule before you type.
5. **Desktop navigation.** The left rail with icons *and* labels, and Speak as a highlighted centre item, is clear and quick.
6. **Practice prompts.** "Not sure what to say? Try one of these" with ready phrases lowers the blank-page anxiety of a speech app.
7. **Kind empty states.** "Spot on –" instead of "0 %" on a day with no practice; "You've practised on 1 day this week."

## Problems and fixes

### P1: fix first

**1. On phones, the garden pushes every task halfway down the screen.**
On every signed-in phone screen the 3D garden fills the top 330 to 400 px of an 844 px screen, then a faded band, and the page's heading starts at about y = 410. On Voice, the first thing you can actually do starts at the very bottom edge; on Me, the first setting is at y = 600. The person has to scroll on every screen to begin, and the most valuable space on a phone is spent on decoration.
*Fix:* on phones, keep the full garden band only on Home (and the public welcome page). Everywhere else, shrink it to a short strip of about 120 px, or show the garden only behind the header. Put the task at the top.

**2. The bottom tab bar has icons but no labels.**
Home, Progress, Speak, Care team and Me appear on phones as five bare icons (a house, a bar chart, a microphone, two people, one person). Older users and people with cognitive changes after a stroke read words far more reliably than icons, and the desktop rail already shows the words.
*Fix:* add the labels under the icons (12 to 13 px is fine there, as on iOS and Android), the same words as the desktop rail.

**3. A crowded phone header, and My voice hidden in "More".**
The header has a sound button, a theme button and a "More" menu; the bottom bar has the five sections. "More" holds My voice, Help, Terms, Privacy, Accessibility and Sign out, so on a phone the voice settings (one of the product's main features) sit in an overflow menu among the legal pages. The header is so full that the "More" button touches the right edge of the screen with no margin.
*Fix:* move My voice to where people will look for it (a clear card on Me, or swap it into the tab bar), move sound and theme into Me, and leave a small menu button in the header for Help, the legal pages and Sign out. Keep a 16 to 20 px margin on both sides.

**4. Every full page load freezes the page for 1 to 3.5 seconds.**
When the garden is built (models decoded, the park laid out, shaders compiled), the main thread is blocked: a tap on "Get started" or a key press in the sign-in form does nothing for that time. People with tremor or slow movements are the ones most likely to tap twice and get confused.
*Fix:* build the garden in steps across several frames (or in a worker), start it after the page is interactive and idle, and compile shaders ahead (`renderer.compileAsync`). Never block a form. Target no freeze over 200 ms.

**5. The "garden sounds are on" note covers content on the first visit to every signed-in page.**
On a phone it sits over the top of the page and in my screenshots it appeared on Home, Speak, Voice, Progress and Me (it shows until the first press or 8 s). It is announced as sound being on, which for someone startled by sound is the wrong order.
*Fix:* sounds off by default; ask once, on Home, with "Play soft garden sounds?" Yes / No. Never overlay content.

### P2: next

**6. The Progress and Home chart has broken details.**
On desktop Home, the bar for a day with practice is drawn over its own day label ("Tu" is covered by the bar). Days with no practice are 6 px tall buttons sitting on small beige pill shapes behind the day names, which look like rendering marks rather than data, and are almost impossible to tap.
*Fix:* keep the bars above the axis line; draw empty days as a thin line on the axis; make the whole day column (bar, gap and label) the button, at least 44 px wide and the full chart height.

**7. Voice setup is long and heavy on a phone.**
Setup step 2 is now almost four screens tall on a phone: six voices with play buttons, five sliders and the voice-banking upload. For a first-run step, that's a lot of decisions before the person has heard the app do anything.
*Fix:* in setup, show only the voice list (with the accent ordering) and one "Fine-tune" link that opens the sliders; leave voice banking for the Voice page. Keep "Skip for now" visible at the top as well as the bottom.

**8. Confirming every phrase is tiring.**
After each phrase the person is asked if it was right, and fixing it means typing. For someone with a motor impairment, that cost on every phrase is the biggest usability problem in daily use.
*Fix:* when the recogniser is confident, speak straight away and show a small "Not right?" link instead of a question. When it isn't, offer the top two or three readings as large buttons before any keyboard.

**9. The mist behind the words is busy.**
The text column sits on a semi-transparent garden: you can see benches, people, lamp posts and paving through it (desktop Home and Speak show people and a signpost ghosted behind the stats card and the microphone). It passes the contrast tests, but it adds visual noise exactly where people read.
*Fix:* blur the garden under the text column (a CSS `backdrop-filter: blur(8px)` on the mist layer, or render the scene at lower resolution there), or raise the mist slightly only behind running text.

**10. Placeholder contact details are live.**
The footer shows `hello@epakovoice.example` and `+44 20 7946 0958` (a UK drama number). Judges and users will notice; a phone number is also an odd first choice for people with a speech difficulty.
*Fix:* real addresses before the demo, email and a contact form first, phone last.

**11. Speak on desktop feels empty and off-centre.**
The microphone sits top-left in a wide screen with the footer pulled up into the lower third, and a hard edge between the garden and the footer.
*Fix:* give Speak the full viewport height (no footer on this screen), and centre the microphone in the text column vertically.

**12. The camera walk takes 3 seconds every time.**
It is lovely the first time. If someone moves between Progress and Me several times, they watch it again each time, and the "ready" state (used by tests and the still picture) waits for it.
*Fix:* shorten it to about 1.2 s for sections visited before in the session, and make it instant under reduced motion (check that it already is).

### P3: polish

13. **Header "Help" link is 37 px wide.** It passes WCAG 2.2 (24 px), but for this audience aim for 44 × 44; add horizontal padding.
14. **Progress filters wrap into one muddle on phones.** "Phrases / Spot on / Minutes" and "Week / 4 weeks" are two separate choices, but at 390 px the second group wraps under the first and reads as one row. Put them on separate lines with a small label each.
15. **The skip link is 40 × 24 px.** Fine for keyboard users; make it full-width when focused.
16. **The radio buttons in the voice list are drawn at 13 to 20 px.** The whole row is clickable, which is right; make the circle itself at least 24 px so the selected state is easy to see.
17. **Day labels in the chart ("Th", "Fr") are abbreviations.** Use single letters with the full name for screen readers (already there), or full short names on desktop.
18. **The far city and crowds look game-like up close.** Fine through the mist; avoid camera stops that look straight at them.
19. **Garden sounds and theme toggles on public pages.** Theme only is right there; good. On signed-in phone screens see item 3.

## Mobile, in short

What's right: nothing overflows, text is never under 16 px, inputs are big and don't zoom, the primary buttons are full-width, the bottom bar keeps Speak one tap away from anywhere, and reduced motion and Save-Data both turn the 3D scene off.

What's wrong: half of each screen is decoration (1), tab icons without words (2), a crowded header touching the edge with My voice hidden in "More" (3), a 1 to 2.4 s freeze on load (4), and an overlay on first visits (5). I did not test on a real phone, with a real thumb, on mobile data, or with the on-screen keyboard open (check that the sign-in button stays visible above the keyboard).
